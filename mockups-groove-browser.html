Groove browser — the scale selector's own design

Second pass, matched to the scale picker piece for piece: the same narrow frame, Built-in | My grooves where Library | My pans sits, the categories where Common · Rare · All sit with the ♥ last in the row as it is there, full-bleed rows with hairline rules, ✓ and amber on the chosen one. And the picker's best idea carried over: a family is one row with variation chips, the way one scale is one row with build chips — 43 grooves become 24 rows. The ♥ marks the family, as it marks the scale. Everything works: filter, switch variations, heart, choose.

Choices this pass makes, so you can overturn them

♥ sits last in the filter row, because that is where the scale picker puts it — your "favourites tab at the top" is the row itself; say the word and it moves to the front. A groove family is one row — hearting it favourites the family, and the chips choose the variation, exactly as a scale row's chips choose the build. The subtitle names the meter and how many variations the family holds. ▸ would play the groove on the current pan, as it previews a scale — a small win the old browser never had.

What building it involves

The picker's CSS is reused nearly verbatim (this page's styles are lifted from it), a cats:[…] field joins the coll the grooves already carry, favourites store as hps.grooveFavs beside the pan favourites, and the family rows come from the patGroups() the browser already computes. The chosen filter is remembered so the browser reopens where you left it.
