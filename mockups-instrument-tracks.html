Writing the instruments, one at a time

Option C. A written part is hits: moments where the instrument plays its chord in a chosen form — a block chord, a roll up or down, a strum down or up, a muted chuck. The notes follow the chord, as the bass’s written notes do. A bar with anything written plays exactly what is written; a bar with nothing written plays the style — and the first hit you write in a bar writes out what the instrument was already playing there, so you start from what you hear. Only the instrument you are writing shows its lane.

cAn arpeggio, written

“C will work for arpeggios as well?” — yes. A roll is one hit with a start, a direction and a length; its notes come from the chord. Bar 2 of the piano, drawn large: the selected roll can move (← →), turn round (↑ ↓), grow or shrink (the length), or climb or drop a chord tone (the height). What it cannot do is change one note inside it.

To decide on this mockup

  1. The lane: taller than the bass’s (58 px against 44), dots for the notes, a roll as a climbing or falling run. Readable?
  2. The forms: piano Block · Roll ↑ · Roll ↓; guitar Strum ↓ · Strum ↑ · Block · Roll ↑ · Roll ↓ · Muted. Anything missing?
  3. Length in sixteenths (3–6), with the rule that a roll reaches at least the next beat — or would you rather choose “to the next beat · 1 beat · 2 beats”?
  4. The synth would take the piano’s palette, with Held · Pulse in place of the rolls. Right?
