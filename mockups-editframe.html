“You are editing” — frame study

Seven ways for the notation to say it is being written on, none of them loud. Each card below is the real score card with the real tokens; only the frame changes. The first pair shows reading and editing side by side, which is the only comparison that matters — the cue has to be obvious when you glance up, and invisible when you are not looking for it.

Unmissable, and the one I would argue against: the paper is the notation's constant, and tinting it makes the music look like a different document rather than the same one held differently. It also fights the ghost notes and the count line for contrast.

4 — a ring outside the frame my suggestion

The card keeps its brown rim exactly as it reads when playing; a soft amber halo sits outside it. Nothing inside the frame moves, so the notation is untouched and the layout does not shift by a pixel — it is a shadow, not a border. It is the clearest of these at a glance and the easiest to ignore while reading, and it costs no width, which matters on the phone where the card is already the full screen.

7 — steel, for comparison

The same ring in the app's blue. Cooler and calmer, and it does not collide with amber's existing meaning of “sounding now” — which is the one real argument against 4. If the playhead and the editing cue ever look confusable to you, this is the answer.

The one trade to decide. Amber already means “this is sounding” in the app — the playhead box, the loop frame, an engaged toggle. Using it for “this is editable” asks the same colour to carry a second meaning. In practice they never appear in the same place (the marker is inside the cells, the cue is on the rim), which is why 4 still gets my vote; but if you would rather keep amber for sound alone, 7 is the same idea in steel and costs nothing to switch.
