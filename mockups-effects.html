The grace, drawn by your rule

The grace stands above the stroke it leads into, in the other hand's colour, nudged left. In the Table it goes to the top of its stack. In the Tab every stroke stands in its own hand's lane, the grace too — a left-hand grace on the top row, its right-hand main on the bottom — and the nudge alone says which leads. When it gets tight

The nudge is the room the slot has. A grace steps left by a third of a glyph when the slot can spare it, by less when it cannot, and not at all when the step would be too small to read — then the flam is a plain chord, and the table is untouched. One measurement per table decides it, the same way the fit already decides the zoom.

The rows below are that rule running in this page, not a drawing of it: each row measures its own slot and glyph and sets the nudge. Slot pitch from left to right: the phone's one bar to a row, then narrower, down to two bars to a row.

The roll, drawn

A roll is a short grace-note roll into the same stroke: the app already plays it (a quiet stroke 45 ms before the accent) and already draws it, but only on percussion, as a tilde over the T. Your own tables put the tilde over a ding as well, which the app cannot draw today. The rule: any note can roll, and a rolling note wears a tilde above it in the other hand's colour — the grace stroke is the other hand's, as it is in a flam.

The tilde is drawn as a mark above the glyph rather than typed as the combining character the app uses now, so it keeps its size and place at every density and on two-digit numbers. Same look, steadier.

The roll, edited

  1. Effects › Roll on the phone, the Roll key or R on the desktop. It arms and lights amber; the pop-up closes, the button shows the dot.
  2. Tap the note. It lands with the tilde; the arming goes out. One-shot, like Ghost and Flam.
  3. Flam and Roll cannot both wait for the same note: arming one disarms the other. A rolled note can still be part of a flam's stack — arm Roll, tap the main; arm Flam, tap the grace.
  4. To take a roll off, clear the slot and place the note again — the editor's one way of changing any note, so nothing new to learn.

The dot has gone out because the roll has been spent. Sound: the roll's grace is the same note, 45 ms before, at 45 %; the flam's grace is the other note, 30 ms before, at 55 %. Both numbers are yours to tune.

How a flam is written

  1. Tap the main note on the pan. It lands on the cursor's slot as today.
  2. Effects › Flam. The pop-up closes on the tap; the button shows the amber dot.
  3. Tap the grace note. It joins the slot's stack carrying flam:true, drawn nudged; the dot goes out. One grace per flam, so Flam is a one-shot like Ghost.

The grace's hand follows Alt in the Table, as you said. In the Tab the two lanes are the hands, so the grace simply stands in the lane of the hand it was written with, nudged left — nothing extra to choose. A same-note D-into-D flam is two taps on the ding with Alt on.
