The Effects pop-up

The phone's lower bar keeps its place and its size. Alt, the Open · Mute · Ovt switch and the new Flam move into one pop-up, and its button stands exactly where the switch stood.

Drawn at the deck's real geometry on a 393px phone: 361px track, 5px gaps, 12px chips. The pop-up wears the same clothes as the header's room drop-down (card, notch, lit row in amber), opened upward because the bar is at the bottom.

When anything inside is not at its default — Flam or Roll armed, Mute or Ovt selected, Alt off — the button carries an amber edge and dot, so a closed pop-up never hides a state that will change the next note.

How a flam is written

  1. Tap the main note on the pan. It lands on the cursor's slot as today.
  2. Effects › Flam. The pop-up closes on the tap; the button shows the amber dot.
  3. Tap the grace note. It joins the slot's stack carrying flam:true, drawn nudged; the dot goes out. One grace per flam, so Flam is a one-shot like Ghost.

The grace's hand follows Alt in the Table, as you said. In the Tab the two lanes are the hands, so the grace simply stands in the lane of the hand it was written with, nudged left — nothing extra to choose. A same-note D-into-D flam is two taps on the ding with Alt on.
